# Executive Financial Briefing – FY 2025
**Prepared for:** Chief Financial Officer
**Period:** January – December 2025

---

**Overall Financial Health & Margin Trends**

The business demonstrated generally healthy net margins throughout 2025, with performance ranging from a low of 29.44% in July to a high of 44.49% in October. The year opened solidly at 38.86% in January and showed meaningful improvement through mid-year, peaking in June at 43.64% — driven in part by notably lower expenses of $24,029.28 against revenue of $42,637.01. However, two soft patches are worth flagging: July's margin compression to 29.44% and December's year-end dip to 33.55%, the latter coinciding with the year's highest expense figure of $26,541.23. October stands out as the strongest month overall, combining peak revenue of $46,586.92 with a 44.49% margin, suggesting favorable operating leverage when revenue scales.

**Notable Revenue Growth & Decline Months**

Three months warrant specific CFO attention due to sharp revenue swings. March recorded a month-over-month decline of -10.78%, falling from $42,632.48 to $38,038.31 — the cause of this early-year pullback should be investigated, whether seasonal, demand-driven, or tied to execution gaps. July was more severe, posting a -12.91% decline from $42,637.01 to $37,131.59, representing the year's lowest revenue figure and a simultaneous spike in expenses to $26,201.13 — a double pressure on profitability. November then recorded the steepest single-month drop of -14.47%, falling from the year's peak of $46,586.92 to $39,846.41. While October's strength may have pulled forward demand, the magnitude of November's reversal deserves scrutiny. On the positive side, August's recovery of 13.18% growth and October's 10.49% surge demonstrate the business's capacity for strong rebounds.

**Departmental Budget Variances Requiring Attention**

Several departments recorded budget overruns significant enough to warrant investigation. Operations in October exceeded budget by 18.79%, with actual spend of $18,759.13 against a budget of $15,791.42 — the largest single-department overage of the year. Marketing also showed a pattern of recurring overruns, most notably in October at 15.83% over budget ($18,841.61 actual vs. $16,266.52 budgeted) and April at 15.77% over ($18,206.05 vs. $15,725.52 budgeted). Engineering exceeded budget by 15.01% in October ($18,519.22 vs. $16,102.01) and by 11.75% in February ($17,321.94 vs. $15,501.18), suggesting potential resource or project cost control issues in that department. Notably, October saw all four departments simultaneously exceed their budgets, which coincided with the revenue peak — leadership should clarify whether this spend drove the revenue outperformance or represents an efficiency concern. On the favorable side, Operations in July came in 12.53% under budget, and Sales in March ran 9.77% below budget, both of which may reflect activity slowdowns consistent with the revenue declines observed in those months.
